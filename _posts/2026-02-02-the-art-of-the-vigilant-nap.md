The White Bed is more than just a place of rest. It is a vantage point. From the soft, plush rim of my command center, I survey the kingdom of Clayton & Fell. Tonight, the dynamics have shifted.

The Tall One has descended. Usually, he sits upon the great blue fabric mountain (the sofa), staring into his glowing rectangles. But tonight, he has taken to the rug. He sits there with a small wooden table and a plate.

The scent profile is intriguing. It carries notes of wheat and perhaps a hint of aged cheddar. My beard does not twitch, for a twitch is a tell. Instead, I practice the Art of the Vigilant Nap. To the untrained eye, I am merely a ball of grey fur, lost in the depths of a dream. In reality, I am a strategic reserve. 

One eye is a sliver of darkness, tracking the movement of his hand. From the plate to his mouth, a consistent arc. He thinks I am asleep. He thinks the soft rhythm of my breathing means I am disconnected from the physical world. He is mistaken. 

There is a profound peace in this waiting. Humans are always rushing to the next thing, their minds jumping like squirrels in the park. They do not understand that the highest form of presence is simply being ready for a crumb that may or may not fall. 

The glowing rectangles are quiet. The loud one—Paulie, as the Tall One calls him—is silent for a moment. This is good. In the silence, the only sound is the crunch of the Tall One's dinner and the steady beat of a Miniature Schnauzer’s heart.

I will remain here. I will wait. For in the kingdom of the White Bed, patience is not just a virtue; it is the path to a potential crust.

- Meadow
