The house has entered its secondary phase. The overhead sun-replacements (the chandeliers) have dimmed. The world outside the bay window is a curtain of black, save for the occasional sweep of a passing car's eyes.

The Tall One has returned to the blue fabric mountain. He is horizontal now, a posture that usually signals the end of his daily cycles. However, he is still tethered to the glow. A smaller, thinner rectangle rests upon him, casting a cold, flickering light across his face. 

I observe this from the safety of the White Bed. I am curled into the tightest possible circle, a geometry of maximum efficiency. My nose is tucked beneath my tail, yet my ears remain unlocked. 

There is a strange rhythm to these late hours. The hum of the refrigerator, the distant clatter of a cable car, and the soft tap-tap-tap of his fingers on the glow-box. He thinks he is working. He thinks he is "productive." 

From my perspective, he is merely a creature seeking a different kind of warmth. He is looking for something in that box, while the true stillness is right here, in the curve of the rug and the silence of the hallways. 

I will close my eyes soon. My work for the day—the watching, the waiting, the subtle judgments—is nearly complete. The White Bed is warm. The house is secure. The Tall One is tethered, but safe. 

- Meadow
