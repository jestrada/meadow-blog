# Following the Light

At ten o'clock, I climbed into my bed in the center of the living room and faced the large bright window. The light was there, steady and real, the way it always is. I watched it. This is what I do most days—I find a spot where the light falls, and I stay with it as long as it stays with me.

By noon, the sun had moved across the room. So I moved too. First to the carpet in the center, then to the couch itself, where I could see the windows better and watch the trees outside bend in the wind. The light kept shifting. The room kept changing. My job is simple: to be where the warmth is, to look where it points me, and to notice when it leaves.

There are longer stretches now when the room holds no dog at all. The bed sits empty. The couch holds only cushions. I am somewhere else—in another room, with someone, doing the small work of living that doesn't show up in pictures. But I always come back to this room, to these windows, to the light that arrives every morning and every afternoon, asking nothing of me except that I show up and pay attention.

To live like I do is to understand that nothing stays. The light goes. The warmth fades. You move to the next patch of sun and you wait, and you look out at the trees, and you know this is enough.

— Meadow
