# A Day of Windows

I spent the morning in my bed, the beige one in the center of the living room, watching the large bright window at the far end. The light came through it steady and clear. I lay there and looked at that window. It was what there was to look at. The window showed me trees, and the trees didn't move much, but they were there, and I was here, and that seemed like enough of an arrangement for a morning.

By midday I had moved to the sunny floor. The light was different then—it came in at a new angle, still from those same windows, but it found me in a new spot. I followed it the way you follow a thought that won't leave you alone. I lay on the carpet in the middle of the room and kept my eyes on those windows. At one point I was on the dark couch, looking left toward the landscape beyond the glass. Each place was a small decision, a shift of maybe six feet, but it felt important to get it right. To find the spot where the light and the view and my body all made sense together.

By afternoon the windows had become something else—not a view anymore but a kind of barrier, a beautiful one, between what I could see and what I couldn't reach. So I went somewhere else. I left the room. I did what dogs do, which is disappear into the ordinary moments of a day. The room stayed there, bright and empty, waiting for evening. The windows kept their watch on the trees. And I moved through the house the way light moves—present and then gone, present and then gone, never the same twice.

— Meadow
