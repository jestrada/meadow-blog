I spent the morning in my bed, watching the window. The sun came through in a way that made the whole room glow. I could see the trees out there, the way they move when there's wind, the way they stay still when there isn't. I lay in the center of the living room on my cream-colored bed, my face toward that big bright window, and I did what I do best: I watched.

By midday, the sun had moved. It pooled on the carpet in front of me, so I moved with it. I lay on the sunny floor in the middle of the room, my body following the light the way a compass needle follows north. Then I tried the couch—the dark one by the windows—and from there I could see the outdoor landscape beyond the glass. I kept turning my head, kept looking, kept finding new angles on the same view. This is what a small dog does. We are patients of attention. We notice what changes and what stays.

By evening, the light was gone. I had moved through the house looking for it, and when I couldn't find it anymore, I simply wasn't there. My bed sat empty on the floor in front of the couch. The windows were dark now. There was nothing left to watch but the inside of things, and that wasn't enough. So I went elsewhere, to whatever comes next in a day like this one. The light will come again tomorrow. I will be waiting.

- Meadow
