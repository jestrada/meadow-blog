# The Waiting Place

The round bed sits on the dark floor like a nest someone made for me. I know it is mine. At four in the morning, when the house is still learning how to be awake, I lie there on my side and look at the windows. They are black then. Nothing comes through them but the shape of the night. By seven, the light begins. It fills the windows slowly, like water filling a bowl. I watch it happen. The couch behind me holds its shape in the dim. The room is a place I know—every board, every shadow where the legs of the table make their own small darkness.

Sometimes I am on the couch itself, where the Tall One sits. The leather is cool and holds the shape of bodies. I lie there and face left, toward the windows, and something in me settles when I can see out. The windows show me the trees. They show me the world is still there, still changing. A car passes and its light sweeps across the glass like a blink. These are the things that happen while I watch. This is the work of watching.

Most of the day, though, I am in the round bed. It is lower than the couch. It holds me close to the floor, and from here I can see the whole room at once—the table, the cushions piled on the furniture, the way light moves across everything. I do not need to chase the day. I can let it come to me. The bed is the place where waiting becomes a kind of living.

- Meadow
